## § INTERVIEW BRIEF

### Five most likely technical questions

**Q1. Walk me through how you'd design an EVE sensitivity calculation for a Canadian Schedule I bank's banking book under the OSFI B-12 standard shocks.**
EVE is the present value of all banking-book asset, liability, and off-balance-sheet cashflows under a given rate scenario; sensitivity is the delta between the base curve and each prescribed shock (parallel up/down, steepener, flattener, short-rate up/down). The mechanics I've operated at Moody's — curve construction, repricing behaviour, behavioral cashflow overlays, cross-asset spread treatment — are the same building blocks. The bank-specific layer I'd want to discuss with the team is deposit beta and non-maturity deposit (NMD) behaviouralization, since those drive the answer more than the curve mechanics themselves.

**Q2. How do you model non-maturity deposit (NMD) behaviour, and what's your view on the risk of mis-specifying the runoff profile?**
NMDs are the dominant modelling-judgment input to NII and EVE in a retail-deposit-heavy book — runoff assumptions, rate-pass-through (deposit beta), and the cap on behavioral maturity (B-12 has prescribed caps) all flex the answer materially. My honest framing: my direct depth is on the asset-side and institutional behavioral cashflows (prepayment, callable structures) rather than retail NMD specifically — but the validation logic is identical: back-test the assumptions against realized data, stress them in both directions, and escalate if the model's sensitivity to the assumption is high.

*(STAR Story 2)* A client-delivery run produced portfolio-level sensitivities that passed all internal checks but didn't square with the client's economic intuition under a specific rate-shock scenario.
