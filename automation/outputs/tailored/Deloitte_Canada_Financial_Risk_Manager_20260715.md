### 5 most likely technical questions

**1. "Walk me through how you'd design a liquidity stress-testing framework for a bank client."**
I've built exactly this at Moody's — an enterprise multi-asset cash-flow projection engine with time-bucketed liquidity gap analytics from T+1 through multi-year horizons, running base, stress, and reverse-stress scenarios. The key is embedding behavioral cash-flow assumptions and prepayment logic so the projection reflects depositor and asset behavior under stress, not just contractual maturities — then layering macro overlays for contingency funding planning. (STAR Story 1)

**2. "How do you measure IRRBB, and what's the difference between EVE and NII sensitivity?"**
I oversee IRRBB analytics under parallel and non-parallel rate shocks aligned with Basel Committee frameworks. EVE captures the change in economic value of equity — a present-value, long-horizon view sensitive to duration mismatch across the whole balance sheet — while NII sensitivity is the near-term earnings view over a rolling horizon. A defensible framework reports both because they can point in different directions depending on repricing structure. Non-parallel shocks (steepeners, flatteners, short-rate shocks) often expose risks a parallel shock masks.

**3. "Tell me about a time you challenged a model output."**
A client-delivery run produced portfolio-level sensitivities that passed every internal check but didn't square with the client's economic intuition under a specific rate shock. I held the release, decomposed the sensitivities by asset class, and isolated a curve-calibration edge case in short-end inversion handling. I escalated to product owners and the client's Head of Risk with a remediation plan — a 48-hour delay that prevented the client acting on wrong numbers. (STAR Story 2)

**4. "You're at a vendor now — how does that translate to consulting delivery?"**
My Moody's role is client-facing at three layers: onboarding institutional clients, translating investment-team requirements into product/dev workstreams, and signing off on the analytics they consume. Before that, EY and Ortec were pure advisory — IFRS 17 transformation across finance/actuarial/IT, and ALM studies presented to pension investment committees. The consulting muscle — scoping, stakeholder alignment, delivery under deadline — is continuous across all three. (STAR Stories 3, 5)

**5. "Describe a regulatory-remediation or transformation project you delivered end-to-end."**
