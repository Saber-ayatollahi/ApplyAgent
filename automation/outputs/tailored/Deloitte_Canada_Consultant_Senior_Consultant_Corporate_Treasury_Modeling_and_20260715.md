## § INTERVIEW BRIEF

**5 most likely technical questions + model answers:**

1. **"Walk us through how you'd build a liquidity stress-testing / cash-flow projection model for a bank balance sheet."**
   I built exactly this at Moody's — an enterprise multi-asset cash-flow projection engine supporting base, stress, and reverse-stress scenarios across ALM horizons. The key design choices are time-bucketing (T+1 through multi-year) for liquidity-gap visibility, embedding behavioral assumptions and prepayment logic rather than contractual-only cash flows, and layering macro stress overlays so the same engine serves both BAU and contingency planning. I re-engineered the upstream workflow into auditable Python with logging and validation so it survives model-governance review.

2. **"How do you approach validating an ALM model you didn't build?"** *(STAR Story 2 & 6)*
   I operate within a formal model-governance framework at Moody's with independent review-and-challenge. Beyond checking that outputs are mathematically defensible, I test economic defensibility — I once held a client release where sensitivities passed every internal check but contradicted the client's economic intuition under a rate shock; decomposing by asset class surfaced a short-end curve-calibration edge case that I escalated and got remediated upstream. Validation is about catching the economically-wrong-but-technically-passing case.

3. **"Explain structural interest-rate risk in the banking book and how you'd measure it."**
   Structural IRRBB is the sensitivity of both economic value (EVE) and earnings (NII) to rate moves arising from repricing and duration mismatch between assets and liabilities. At Moody's I oversee duration and rate-shock analysis under both parallel and non-parallel shifts — non-parallel matters because curve twists and short-end inversions expose mismatches parallel shocks miss. Behavioral assumptions on non-maturity deposits and prepayment optionality are where most of the modelling judgment lives.

4. **"You mention Fund Transfer Pricing in the JD — what's your experience?"** *(GAP — handle honestly)*
   My direct hands-on has been on the IRRBB, liquidity-gap, and balance-sheet-aggregation side rather than running an FTP framework end-to-end. That said, FTP is the pricing layer that sits on the same curve-construction and behavioral-cash-flow machinery I work in daily — the funding curve, liquidity premium, and behavioral term assumptions are all inputs I already model. I'd ramp on the specific transfer-pricing conventions quickly given that foundation.

5. **"How do you use Python in your modelling work, and where does Excel/VBA still fit?"**
   Python is my production layer — pandas/NumPy/SciPy pipelines with embedded logging, validation, and auditability, which I built by migrating spreadsheet workflows into version-controlled code under governance audit. Excel and VBA remain the client-facing and prototyping layer; I read and refactor legacy VBA macros regularly. I also use agentic-AI tooling (Claude Code, Cursor) to accelerate first-pass code review while keeping human sign-off on anything governance-critical.
