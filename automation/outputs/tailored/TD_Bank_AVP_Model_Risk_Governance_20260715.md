## § INTERVIEW BRIEF

### 5 most likely technical questions + model answers

**1. "Walk me through how you'd structure an enterprise Model Risk Policy and framework."**
Anchor on tiering: models classified by materiality and risk, with proportional validation, documentation, and monitoring requirements. At Moody's I operate inside a framework where sign-off authority is *delegated by role, not title*, review depth scales with model criticality, and every output must be economically defensible, not just mathematically valid. An enterprise policy formalizes that — inventory, ownership, validation cadence, exception handling, and escalation — with the framework, procedures, standards, and templates all consistent.

**2. "Tell me about a time you challenged a model output others were ready to accept."** *(STAR Story 2)*
A client-delivery run produced portfolio sensitivities that passed all internal checks but contradicted the client's economic intuition under a specific rate shock. Under deadline pressure I held the release, decomposed sensitivities by asset class, and traced it to a curve-calibration edge case (short-end inversion handling). I escalated to product owners and the client's Head of Risk with a remediation plan. Release slipped 48 hours; the defect was fixed upstream and captured in validation tests — and I became the Head of Risk's direct escalation contact.

**3. "How do you think about model-lifecycle controls and auditability?"** *(STAR Story 6)*
Controls have to survive audit, which means logging, versioning, and reproducibility — not tribal knowledge in a spreadsheet. I migrated a spreadsheet-driven valuation workflow into a Python pipeline by parallel-building it, running it in shadow mode for two cycles, reconciling output, then cutting over with a rollback plan. The governance audit closed satisfactorily and the pattern became a template. Enterprise inventory systems need the same discipline at scale.

**4. "You reference SR 11-7 and OSFI E-23 — how do they shape governance? What about SR26-2?"**
SR 11-7 establishes the three pillars — development/implementation soundness, ongoing monitoring, and independent validation — plus effective challenge and governance/documentation. E-23 extends model-risk expectations for Canadian institutions including AI/ML coverage and functional separation. I'd be transparent that SR26-2 is one I'd want to read in full before speaking to its specifics; my working approach is to map any new supervisory guidance onto the existing three-pillar and functional-separation architecture rather than treat it as a bolt-on.

**5. "How do you communicate model risk to a Board or non-technical executive?"** *(Ortec IC experience)*
I translate quantitative outputs into decision-relevant narratives — at Ortec I presented ALM methodology to pension investment committees, and at Moody's I prepare risk summaries for senior stakeholders. The discipline is to lead with the risk decision the audience faces, quantify the exposure in their language, then support with methodology only if asked. For Board/regulator reporting that means a clean model-risk profile, adherence status by business unit, and clearly flagged exceptions.
