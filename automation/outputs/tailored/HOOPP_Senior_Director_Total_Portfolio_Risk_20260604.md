## § INTERVIEW BRIEF

### Five most likely technical questions

**1. "Walk us through how you'd structure a total-portfolio risk framework for a pension plan with mature liabilities."**
Start from the surplus, not the asset side. Liabilities modelled as a duration-and-inflation-sensitive cash-flow stream; assets aggregated to the same risk-factor lens (rates, credit, equity, FX, inflation, illiquidity). Measure on the surplus: funding-ratio volatility, VaR/CVaR on surplus, contribution-to-risk by factor and by asset class. At Ortec I built exactly this for Canadian pension clients using GLASS — including the UPP three-plan merger where Funding Policy dynamics drove the modelling choices.

**2. "VaR vs. CVaR — when do you prefer one, and how do you handle the well-known weaknesses?"**
CVaR / Expected Shortfall for any decision where tail behavior matters — which is most pension decisions, because the liability is itself a long-tail object. VaR is fine for high-frequency, well-behaved exposures but is non-coherent and silent on tail shape. Practically: I run both in parallel, decompose CVaR by factor, and pair it with stress and reverse-stress scenarios so the number doesn't have to carry the whole story. At Ortec I used CVaR optimization on the surplus and then ran near-optimal-frontier analysis to test the robustness of the recommendation — that addresses the parameter-instability weakness.

**3. "Tell me about a time a risk number was technically right but economically wrong, and what you did."**
*[STAR Story 2.]* Client-delivery run at Moody's where sensitivities passed every internal check but didn't square with the client's intuition under a specific rate-shock scenario. Held the release under deadline pressure, decomposed the sensitivities by asset class, found a curve-calibration edge case in short-end inversion handling, escalated to product owners and the client's Head of Risk. Release delayed 48 hours; defect remediated upstream; became the Head of Risk's direct escalation contact afterward.

**4. "How would you think about risk decomposition for a portfolio with meaningful illiquid allocations — private equity, infrastructure, real estate?"**
The honest answer is that illiquids break naïve VaR/CVaR because smoothed valuations understate true exposure. I un-smooth the return series, then map exposures to underlying risk factors rather than asset-class buckets — infrastructure carries duration, inflation linkage, and equity-like residual; private equity carries levered public-equity beta plus financing exposure. Then run contribution-to-risk on the factor-level decomposition. At Ortec I did the factor-mapping approach for client portfolios where private allocations would otherwise have flattered the risk number.

**5. "How do you challenge a CIO or investment committee that's pushing back on a risk finding?"**
*[STAR Story 4 + sign-off framing from Story 9.]* The trust is built before the meeting — by being right on the boring stuff first. When the pushback comes, decompose the disagreement: is it the assumption, the model, or the interpretation? Bring the decomposition into the room rather than the conclusion.
