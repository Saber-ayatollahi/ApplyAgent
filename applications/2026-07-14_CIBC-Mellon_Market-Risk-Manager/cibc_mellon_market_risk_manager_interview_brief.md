## Likely technical questions

**1. How do you measure and challenge interest-rate risk on a fixed-income book?**
Walk through duration/key-rate duration and convexity, then parallel and non-parallel curve shocks. At Moody's I oversee this analysis and validate the sensitivities at portfolio-level aggregates. As second line, my challenge focuses on curve-construction choices and whether short-end/inversion handling is economically defensible (Story 2).

**2. Give an example of independent review that changed an outcome.**
Use Story 2: a client run passed all internal checks but sensitivities didn't square with economic intuition under a rate shock. I held the release, decomposed by asset class, found a curve-calibration edge case (short-end inversion), escalated to the client's Head of Risk, and drove an upstream fix captured in validation tests — delayed 48 hours, avoided acting on wrong numbers.

**3. How do you approach fixed-income valuation and risk sensitivities?**
Curve construction and calibration, spread calibration, cross-asset interactions, then sensitivity consistency checks. I validate rates/FX/inflation derivatives outputs and confirm sensitivities aggregate coherently from security to portfolio level — the review layer, not the model build.

**4. How do you assess liquidity risk?**
Story 1: I led design of a multi-asset cash-flow projection engine with time-bucketed liquidity gap analytics (T+1 to multi-year) under base, stress, and reverse-stress scenarios, embedding behavioral cash-flow and prepayment assumptions — giving forward-looking liquidity visibility across horizons.

**5. What makes an effective second line of defense?**
Independence, economic defensibility, and clear escalation. Sign-off attests to defensibility of specific outputs, not the business strategy (Story 9). I challenge, document to governance standards, and escalate with a remediation path rather than rubber-stamping.
