## Five likely technical questions

**1. "Walk us through how you would challenge the discount rate in an equity valuation of a revenue-risk infrastructure asset."**
I approach it as a decomposition problem: risk-free curve, then the spread/premium layered on top, then whether the premium is consistent with the cash flow risk actually being priced. At Moody's my sign-off work is exactly this - independently reviewing curve construction and spread calibration before valuation outputs go to institutional clients. I would test whether the discount rate is internally consistent with the revenue-forecast scenario it is applied to, since double-counting risk in both the numerator and denominator is the most common error.

**2. "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a model output."** (STAR Story 2)
A client-delivery run produced portfolio sensitivities that passed every internal check but contradicted the client's economic intuition under a specific rate shock. Under deadline pressure I held the release, re-ran sensitivities decomposed by asset class, isolated a curve-calibration edge case in short-end inversion handling, and escalated to the product owners and the client's Head of Risk with a remediation plan. Release slipped 48 hours; the client avoided acting on wrong numbers, and I became their direct escalation contact.

**3. "How would you build a standardized valuation methodology and model-review playbook from scratch?"** (Stories 6 and 9)
I sit on Moody's model governance committee, where methodology review, documentation and benchmarking standards are set. My template is: define the review checklist by input class (discount rate, forecast, terminal assumptions, downside case), require assumption documentation with an owner and a challenge record, parallel-run any new approach in shadow mode before cut-over, and keep a defined escalation path for outputs that are mathematically clean but economically indefensible. That last distinction is what governance actually buys you.

**4. "Single-point DCF versus probability-weighted scenarios - when does each belong?"** (Story 4)
At Ortec I built stochastic economic scenario generators and ran funding-ratio distributions under base and stressed regimes, then decomposed which risk drivers contributed most to outcome volatility. A point DCF is a communication device; the distribution is the decision tool. For assets with revenue risk and asymmetric payoffs, a small number of well-argued, probability-weighted states is more defensible than a single central case with a wider discount rate.

**5. "You have sign-off authority as an Assistant Director - explain the scope."** (Story 9)
Moody's delegates sign-off by role within a formal governance framework, not by title. I attest to the defensibility of specific analytical outputs - valuation, sensitivity, ALM - for portfolios of $5-25bn per engagement, not to a client's investment strategy. It is an independent-review authority, and the honest boundary is important: I can hold a release, and I have.

## Three questions to ask

1. For equity and upside-sharing exposures, where does the current valuation debate actually sit - discount rate selection, terminal/exit assumptions, or the probability weights on revenue cases? Which one causes the most friction with deal teams?
2. How does the independent challenge function's view get formalized - is there a written dissent/escalation record that goes to the VP or a committee, or is it resolved in discussion with the investment team?
3. What state are the valuation playbooks in today - is this role writing the first standardized set, or hardening and enforcing something that already exists across the portfolio?
