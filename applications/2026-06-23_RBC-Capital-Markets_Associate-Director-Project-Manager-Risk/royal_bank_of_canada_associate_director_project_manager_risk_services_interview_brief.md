## Top 5 Likely Questions

**1. Walk me through a complex initiative you delivered with shared resources, interdependent milestones, and an aggressive timeline. How did you keep it on track?**
At Moody's I led the design and implementation of a multi-asset cash-flow projection engine. I broke it into time-bucketed workstreams (curve / behavioral assumptions / stress overlays), coordinated dev + QA + BA with a shared backlog in JIRA, and ran weekly status against a phased plan. Where dependencies slipped, I rebaselined and escalated early to the PMO rather than absorbing the slack silently. Engine shipped to production and became a template for adjacent modules.

**2. Give me an example of translating complex technical content into an executive-level summary.**
During a client-delivery cycle, portfolio sensitivities passed internal checks but didn't square with the client's economic intuition under a specific rate shock. I held the release, decomposed sensitivities by asset class, identified a curve-calibration edge case, and produced a one-page summary for the client's Head of Risk and our internal PO covering: what we saw, what it meant economically, the remediation, and the 48-hour timeline. The Head of Risk became my direct escalation contact after that.

**3. How do you operate in a matrix environment where you don't have direct authority over the people delivering?**
My four years at Moody's and prior EY engagements have all been matrix. The pattern that works: align on the decision-rights map at kickoff, run a single tracked backlog so nobody is fighting over what's in scope, and translate dev pushback into business language for stakeholders (and vice versa). On the Calypso-to-PFaroe migration I bridged a pension client's investment desk and our dev team by scoping requirements into structured PO requests - onboarding hit schedule, pattern was reused for the rest of the migration cohort.

**4. How do you handle reporting upward when a project is at risk?**
Early, structured, and with options. I separate facts (where we are vs. plan), implications (what slips and by how much), and decisions needed (what the PMO has to choose between). I do not bring problems without at least two viable paths. At Moody's I have escalated outputs lacking economic defensibility under deadline pressure - holding the line on quality while giving leadership a clear choice cost me 48 hours once and saved a client relationship.

**5. What's your approach to JIRA / project tracking hygiene across concurrent workstreams?**
One source of truth, ticket-per-deliverable with explicit acceptance criteria, dependencies tagged, and a Friday status snapshot rolled up into the PMO format. I built this discipline at Moody's where I run concurrent client modules with shared engineering resources - if the board isn't clean, the status report isn't trustworthy, and PMO loses confidence in the team.
