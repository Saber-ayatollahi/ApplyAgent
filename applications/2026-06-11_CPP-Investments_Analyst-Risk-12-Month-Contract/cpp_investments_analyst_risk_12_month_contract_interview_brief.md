## Top 5 Likely Technical Questions

**1. "Walk us through how you'd assess risk on a multi-asset portfolio you've never seen before."**
Start with mandate and liability/objective context, then exposures by asset class and factor (rates, credit spread, FX, equity beta), then sensitivities (duration, key-rate, delta, vega where relevant), then aggregate measures (VaR/CVaR with method caveats), then scenario and reverse-stress overlays. At Moody's I do exactly this sequence — security-level exposures aggregated into portfolio-level metrics, then challenged against economic intuition. (Story 2)

**2. "What's the difference between VaR and CVaR, and when do you prefer each?"**
VaR is the loss threshold at a confidence level; CVaR is the expected loss conditional on breaching that threshold — tail-aware and sub-additive, so it composes properly across sub-portfolios. At Ortec I ran both for asset-only and surplus optimization on GLASS; CVaR is preferred when tails matter (pension surplus, illiquid private exposures) and for risk budgeting where coherence matters.

**3. "Tell me about a time you challenged a model output."**
Use Story 2: client-delivery run passed internal checks but didn't square with the client's economic intuition under a specific rate-shock scenario. Held release, decomposed sensitivities by asset class, found a short-end curve-calibration edge case, escalated to product and the client's Head of Risk, remediated upstream with new validation tests. Release slipped 48 hours; trust was earned.

**4. "How would you think about risk indicators for private market exposures versus public?"**
Public: continuous market data lets you run daily VaR, factor decomposition, liquidity-adjusted stress. Private: stale marks force you to lean on look-through factor exposures, vintage-year analysis, leverage and refinancing risk, and scenario/reverse-stress rather than statistical tails. Ortec's pension ALM work spanned both — and the lesson was that private risk lives in scenarios and concentration, not in covariance matrices.

**5. "Why are you applying to an analyst contract role at your level?"**
Honest answer: substance over title. CPP Investments' Risk department is the best independent-oversight seat in Canada, the work compounds across generations, and a 12-month window is enough to contribute meaningfully and learn the Fund's specific frameworks. Senior rigour, analyst humility — I'm comfortable with both.
