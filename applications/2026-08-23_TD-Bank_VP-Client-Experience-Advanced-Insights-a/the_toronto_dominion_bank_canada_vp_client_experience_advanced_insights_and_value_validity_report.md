## What was wrong and what was fixed

**1. JD-domain conflation (biggest issue).** This JD is a retail/consumer Client Experience role (VOC, complaints, NPS/promoter growth, attrition, CX economics, NLP). Saber's entire evidence base is institutional ALM/portfolio-risk analytics. The draft's opening summary line — 'bringing directly relevant capability' — overclaimed domain fit. Fixed to 'bringing transferable capability,' which is honest: the *mechanics* (sign-off, governance, escalation, AI-enablement) transfer; the *domain* (VOC/CX/customer behaviour) does not, and the cover letter now says so explicitly rather than papering over it.

**2. Verb inflation on governance bullet.** Draft said Saber 'sits on the model governance committee, setting methodology review... standards.' Repo only supports 'participates in methodology review... standards' (member, not standard-setter). Downgraded 'setting' → 'contributing to' / 'Participates in.'

**3. Scope-creep on escalation bullet.** Draft added 'driving root cause resolution... and internal product owners' to the Asst. Director escalation bullet. The repo's escalation bullet only supports interfacing with Heads of Risk/investment stakeholders — it does not claim ownership of *resolution*, and 'product owners' belongs to a different role/time period (Client Service Specialist, 2022-23), not this bullet. Fixed to 'driving root-cause diagnosis with clients' Heads of Risk and investment stakeholders' — diagnosis is defensible (he identifies why an output is wrong before escalating); 'resolution' and the misplaced 'product owners' reference were removed.

**4. 'Client' ambiguity used to imply customer-behaviour modelling.** The cash-flow bullet said projections reflect 'observed client and portfolio behaviour' — in a CX cover letter this reads as retail-client behavioural analytics, which Saber has never done. His behavioural modelling is institutional-portfolio prepayment/cash-flow behaviour. Fixed to 'observed institutional portfolio behaviour' to remove the ambiguity.

**5. Domain-specificity stripped from an Ortec bullet to sound more like customer-predictive-analytics.** Draft changed the scenario-generator bullet to 'producing predictive distributions of outcomes' (generic, and quietly adjacent to the JD's 'predict client friction' language). Restored the actual domain: 'predictive distributions of interest-rate and funding-volatility outcomes' — still uses the JD's 'predictive' keyword, but doesn't imply customer-outcome prediction he never did.

**6. Wrongly attributed capability in the summary.** 'Root cause analysis... from Ortec Finance' was not well grounded — Ortec's repo evidence is risk decomposition/attribution, not a named 'root cause analysis' practice; the closest true root-cause-adjacent work (escalating economically indefensible outputs) is at Moody's, not Ortec. **8. Missing exact title (Rule 8).** The original summary referenced the role loosely ('VP, Client Experience, Advanced Insights and Value'). Corrected to the exact posting-title string 'VP Client Experience Advanced Insights and Value' verbatim in the summary's opening sentence.

**9. Cover letter — same 'resolution' overclaim fixed** ('own escalation and root cause resolution' → 'escalate and drive root-cause diagnosis'), and restructured into three substantive paragraphs per the 300-350-word / 3-paragraph rule (final count ≈315 words). The honest gap disclosure paragraph (analytics pointed at balance-sheet/portfolio outcomes, not VOC signals) was kept and sharpened — this is the single most important sentence in the whole application and should not be cut, because an interviewer will find the domain gap in the first two minutes regardless.

- **Zero VOC/NPS/complaints/attrition/customer-analytics experience.** There is no repo evidence of surveys, complaint taxonomies, promoter-score modelling, or customer-attrition prediction. This is the real gap; the cover letter names it rather than hoping the reviewer misses it.

Frame it as a transferable *mental model*, not prior CX-economics delivery.
- **This is a stretch application overall** — strong on AI/agentic enablement, governance, sign-off, and executive reporting; weak-to-absent on the JD's core subject matter (client experience/VOC). Position honestly as a cross-domain athlete, not as a CX practitioner.
