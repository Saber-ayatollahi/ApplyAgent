## 5 likely technical questions (with model answers)

**1. "Walk me through how you would assess a multinational client's liquidity position."**
Start with the cash flow projection framework I built at Moody's: project contractual and behavioural cash flows across base, stress, and reverse-stress scenarios, then bucket the gaps from T+1 through multi-year to see where funding is actually tight versus where it just looks tight. Behavioural assumptions — prepayment, rollover, sticky balances — are where most of the answer lives; contractual profiles alone mislead. From there the conversation with the client is about which gaps are structural (solve with a facility or pooling structure) versus cyclical (solve with investment horizon).

**2. "You come from analytics, not sales. How do you originate?"**
My origination has been consultative rather than quota-driven, but the mechanics rhyme. At Ortec I ran ALM studies that ended in investment-committee recommendations — one Canadian pension client adopted a duration extension off my funding-ratio decomposition and came back for follow-on mandates. At EY I built the IFRS 17 advisory product offering and its go-to-market collateral. The pattern is: diagnose the client's actual exposure, quantify it credibly, and the solution conversation follows. What I'd be adding at BNP is the credit and product structuring layer around it.

**3. "Give me an example of translating a complex client requirement into a workable structure."**
Use Story 3: during the Calypso→PFaroe migration a pension client's ALM configuration requirements kept getting lost between their investment desk and our development organisation. I scoped the requirements into structured Product Owner requests, walked the PO through the investment team's decision logic, and translated engineering pushback back into investment language. Client onboarded on schedule and the configuration pattern was reused across the rest of the migration cohort.

**4. "How do you think about risk when structuring — where do you draw the line?"**
Use Story 2. A client delivery run produced sensitivities that passed every internal check but did not square with the client's economic intuition under a specific rate shock. I held the release under deadline pressure, decomposed by asset class, found a short-end curve-calibration edge case, and escalated to the product owners and the client's Head of Risk with a remediation plan. Forty-eight hours of delay versus a client acting on wrong numbers. I became their direct escalation contact afterwards.

**5. "What's your experience with FX and cross-border exposure?"**
At Ortec I ran interest rate, inflation, and currency hedging analysis for international institutional clients across multiple currencies and markets, including the currency and leverage overlay strategies evaluated in the three-plan university pension merger. At Moody's I validate derivatives pricing outputs across rates, FX, and inflation and confirm sensitivity consistency at portfolio aggregates. So: exposure measurement and hedge structuring, yes; executing FX trades on a corporate desk, no.

## The one competency gap to prepare for

**Trade finance product mechanics and credit-proposal ownership.** Saber has no documentary credits, guarantees, receivables/supply-chain finance, or corporate cash management product experience, and has never written a credit proposal or carried a revenue budget. Do not bluff it. Prepared line: "I have not structured a letter of credit or an SCF programme. What I have done is sit with a client's balance sheet, size their liquidity and currency exposure, structure a defensible answer, and carry it through implementation with operations. The product mechanics are learnable in a quarter; the client judgement isn't." Before the interview, read up on UCP 600 basics, receivables purchase versus payables finance, and notional versus physical cash pooling — enough to ask intelligent questions, not to claim expertise.
