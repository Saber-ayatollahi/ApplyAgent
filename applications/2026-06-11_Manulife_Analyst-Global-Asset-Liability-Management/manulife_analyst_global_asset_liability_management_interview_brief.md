## Likely technical questions (with model answers)

**1. Walk us through how you analyze a movement in duration gap and earnings risk after a rate move.**
At Moody's I decompose the move into curve-level drivers (parallel shift vs. twist/butterfly), then attribute the change in duration gap to asset-side repricing, liability-side discounting, and any hedge roll. Earnings risk I run as NII sensitivity under parallel and non-parallel shocks; I cross-check by re-running the cash-flow projection engine and reconciling to the sensitivity model. The story to senior management is always: what moved, why, and whether it is inside tolerance.

**2. Tell us about a time you held the line on a model output under deadline pressure.**
(STAR Story 2.) A client run produced sensitivities that passed internal checks but did not square with the client's economic intuition under a specific rate shock. I held the release, decomposed sensitivities by asset class, and identified a curve-calibration edge case at the short end. Escalated to PO and the client's Head of Risk; release slipped 48 hours, but the client avoided acting on wrong numbers and the defect was fixed upstream.

**3. How would you approach improving an ALM methodology to acquire IFRS 17 economic and accounting risk metrics?**
From my EY work on IFRS 17, the hard part is reconciling the economic balance-sheet view with the accounting CSM mechanics on the same liability cash flows. I would start by mapping the existing methodology's cash-flow building blocks to the IFRS 17 measurement model (BBA / VFA), identify where discount-rate curves and risk-adjustment assumptions diverge between economic and accounting views, and propose a single source of cash flows with two reporting overlays - that minimizes reconciliation breaks and gives clean drivers for senior management.

**4. What does an LDI / hedging program look like for an insurer vs. a pension fund?**
For pensions (Ortec) the objective is funded-status stability - duration-match the liability and hedge inflation/currency where material. For insurers it is more nuanced: the liability is contractual and IFRS 17-measured, so you are hedging both economic duration AND accounting earnings volatility, often with different instruments. Convexity matters more, and credit spread risk on the asset side interacts with the discount curve on the liability side. The hedge ratio decision is a return/risk optimization, not a pure immunization play.

**5. Why this role given your background is heavier on the asset-manager / consulting side than direct insurer ALM?**
Direct General Fund insurer ALM is the one bench I am explicitly looking to add. The transferable spine is strong: delegated sign-off on multi-asset ALM outputs at Moody's, IFRS 17 / IFRS 9 transformation at EY (insurance clients), and pension ALM / LDI at Ortec. The learning curve is on Manulife's specific liability segments and IFRS 17 production reporting - which is exactly the steep curve the JD flags, and which I have ramped on before.
