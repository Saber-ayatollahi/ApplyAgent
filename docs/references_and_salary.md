### 1.3 Priming the reference (script)

> "Hi [name], I'm hoping you're well. I wanted to give you a heads-up: I'm starting an active job search for Director/VP-level roles in Toronto — ALM, IRRBB, model governance at a bank or pension fund, or a Client Solutions / Platform role at a vendor like BlackRock or S&P. I'd like to list you as a reference if you're comfortable — it would come up at the final-round stage, probably 4-10 weeks from now. The roles will lean heavily on my work at [Moody's/EY/Ortec], so I wanted to make sure that's something you'd feel good speaking to."

### 1.4 Before the reference call (what to send the reference)

- The JD for the specific role.
- A one-page brief of how you positioned yourself in the process — so they can reinforce, not contradict.
- The 2-3 specific stories / accomplishments you'd appreciate them highlighting (e.g., "if the conversation comes up, the model-output escalation story is relevant — it came up in my second round with the hiring manager").

### 1.5 After the reference call

- Thank the reference within 4 hours. Close the loop when an offer lands (or if the process ends).
- Never over-use the same reference; cycle through the four archetypes across different processes.

## Part 3 — Negotiation Playbook

### 3.1 Pre-offer (during the process)

- Never give a specific number first. Always a RANGE.
- If asked "What's your current compensation?" — deflect: *"I'd prefer to discuss expectations rather than current comp — my expectation range for this type of role is consistent with Toronto [tier] market."*
- If they push for a specific range, give a 15-20% spread that places your TARGET in the middle: *"For a Director role at a Big 6, I'd expect base in the $210-245K range with a standard Director bonus."*
- Keep competing processes alive. At final round, casually: *"I should mention I'm at final stages with two other firms — both in the Canadian Big 6 / institutional space. I'm happy to share the timing if that's helpful for your process."*

### 3.2 When the offer lands

**Step 1 — Receive with grace.** *"Thank you so much — this is really exciting. I'd like to take 3-5 days to review and come back with a considered response. Is that acceptable?"* Buy time. Never accept or counter on the call.

**Step 2 — Review the offer in full** against the following checklist:
- Base salary
- Target bonus % and payout structure (discretionary vs formulaic)
- LTIP / equity — vesting schedule, cliff, vehicle (RSU / phantom / cash)
- Sign-on bonus (cash) — any clawback?
- Start date flexibility
- Vacation (minimum 4 weeks at Director level)
- Benefits (medical, dental, health spending account)
- Retirement (DC match / DB plan)
- Professional dues (CFA, conferences)
- Notice period / severance / change of control
- Non-compete / garden leave (critical for moves between direct competitors)

**Step 3 — Benchmark to research.** Is this at Target, Floor, or Stretch? What's the gap?

**Step 4 — Counter.** Use this script (customize):

> "Thank you again for the offer — I'm excited about the role. I've done some careful thinking and would like to propose a few adjustments. The base at $X works well for me, but I'd like to close the gap on total comp a little — specifically I'd ask for (a) the base at $[target base], (b) the target bonus percentage lifted to [target bonus], and if (a) isn't flexible, (c) a sign-on bonus of $[15-25k] that would bridge the gap in Year 1. I'm ready to sign today on any version of this that lands us at ~$[target TC] all-in — the rest of the role and the team are where I want to be."

Key rules:
- **Counter ONCE.** Counter twice and you look greedy / indecisive.
- **Anchor high.** Ask for target; accept at floor.
- **Movement levers (in order of ease):** sign-on bonus → vacation → start date → title → base → LTIP → bonus %. Recruiters have more sign-on flexibility than base flexibility.
- **Never** play offers against each other unless both are real and you'd accept either.
- **Never** threaten to walk unless you're prepared to walk.

**Step 5 — Accept.** Get the offer letter with revised terms in writing before resigning. Always.

### 3.3 Specific tactics for Canadian Director-level roles

- **LTIP is usually 3-year vesting** at banks and pensions — negotiate the cliff if possible (request 1-year cliff vs 3-year cliff).
- **Sign-on bonus to offset lost bonus at current employer.** Common and granted if asked. Ask for ~50-100% of your forfeited current-year bonus.
- **Garden leave clauses** — especially at Big 6 for ALM/Treasury roles. Negotiate maximum 3 months paid garden leave max. Longer puts you out of market.
- **Non-compete** — push back hard on any > 6 months. Often recruiter-side is flexible if you ask.
- **Start date** — 4 weeks is standard. Do not commit to 2 weeks even if the recruiter pressures. You need the run-up to prepare.

### 3.4 When to walk

- Offer is materially below floor AND recruiter signals base is fixed.
- Role scope has shifted during the process to something you'd not have applied for.
- Hiring manager seems disengaged in final round.
- Reference calls reveal toxic culture.
- Non-compete is unreasonable and recruiter won't negotiate.

### 3.5 When to say yes fast

- Offer is at or above target.
- Role scope is as discussed.
- Team fit feels right (you've met the team).
- Start date, vacation, title match expectations.
- You've completed the reference check from the employer side (they've called yours, which means they're committed).

## Part 4 — Quick reference: what to say when asked

### "What's your current salary?"
> "I'd prefer to talk about expectations for the role rather than my current comp, which has a mix of factors that may not map cleanly. My expectation range for this type of Director role is in line with Toronto Big 6 / Maple 8 / vendor market — happy to share specifics as we get closer."

### "What are your salary expectations?"
> "For a Director-level role in [ALM / Model Risk / Client Solutions] at [this company type], I'd expect base in the [$X-Y]K range with standard Director-level bonus and LTIP. Open to discussing total comp in more detail once we're aligned on the role scope."

### "Would you take [low number]?"
> "Thank you for being upfront. That's below my expectation range for a Director-level role — but I'd like to understand the full picture. What's the bonus structure, LTIP, and sign-on? And what's the path to moving the base forward?"

### "Can you start in two weeks?"
> "I can commit to four weeks after accepting an offer — I want to close out my current engagements responsibly. Happy to negotiate if there's a specific reason for the urgency."

### "Do you have other offers?"
> Truthful: *"Nothing signed yet. I'm in late stages with one other firm, and I have two recruiter-led processes active."* Or if not: *"Not currently — I've been selective about where I'm applying. This role was one of my top priorities, which is why I'm engaged in the process."*
