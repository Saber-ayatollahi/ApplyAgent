## Full running narrative (current state)

**Ortec Finance — Consultant (right after MSc, Financial Modelling)**

I'm a risk and quantitative-analytics specialist — CFA, with dual master's degrees, one in Financial Modelling and one in Chemical Engineering. For about seven years I've built and validated risk models and analytics for pension funds and institutional investors. It started at Ortec Finance — the fintech behind the ALM and risk platforms many pension plans and insurers run on, including their GLASS system. As a consultant, I had two main responsibilities. First, supporting existing clients — some of the largest pension funds in Canada and the US, including several of Ontario's Maple-8 plans: OMERS, OPTrust, AIMCo, and HOOPP among them. I worked directly with these funds, saw how their balance sheets are built, and did a lot of hands-on consultancy for them — custom scenario design and extensive stress-testing analysis. Second, running standalone, ad-hoc analysis: ALM studies, portfolio analysis, and consultancy on strategy — strategic asset allocation, tactical asset allocation, portfolio optimization, and hedging analysis — for pension funds based in the US and Canada. I built stochastic economic scenario generators to assess funding and interest-rate risk, and did VaR/CVaR portfolio optimization with risk decomposition and attribution. That's where I went deep on asset-owner, total-fund risk. A recurring part of the work was regulatory too — Canadian pension frameworks vary province to province and evolve, so when regulators introduced new rules I helped translate them into the models and validate they behaved as expected before they reached clients.

One project I was especially close to was the onboarding of the University Pension Plan (UPP) — a newly formed jointly-sponsored plan created from the merger of several Ontario university pension plans. I was heavily involved in building and validating the model of their balance sheet and their funding-policy dynamics.

**EY — technology transformation (brief)**

After Ortec, I had a short stint at EY, working on the delivery of a technology transformation for a large US-based insurance company. I realized fairly quickly that this wasn't where I wanted to build — advisory-transformation work wasn't the right fit for me; I wanted to be hands-on with the quantitative modelling. So when I came across the role at Moody's, which was much more aligned with my interests and expertise, I made a deliberate move.

**Moody's Analytics — Assistant Director (client side → analytics delivery)**

At Moody's I was initially hired as Assistant Director, Client Service Specialist. My main responsibility was helping roll out a new regulatory-framework module for a newly acquired Canadian client, and supporting existing and newly acquired US pension clients on PFaroe — our risk and ALM platform. Clients ranged from pension funds to consultancy firms to CIOs, all using the platform to monitor the daily position of their funds, with us generating the risk analytics, ALM analysis, and daily valuation of those positions.

That same regulatory-modelling pattern was central at Moody's. These frameworks vary province to province and change over time — regulators periodically introduce new rules that have to be modelled. I was the one translating those regulatory requirements to the developers, then testing the new models to confirm the implementation behaved as expected, before we rolled them out gradually to clients.

In that role I was essentially the bridge between the client and the product team — translating client needs into development requests, and validating the models behaved correctly and as expected. It was very client-facing: I was on client sites regularly and engaging directly with asset owners and asset managers — firms like Fidelity on the asset side. A great grounding in both the modelling and the relationships.

I then took on a major role in a roughly nine-month project migrating clients from a legacy pricing/valuation system onto a newly built valuation platform. We moved a large number of clients across, ran the dry runs, and validated the new system was working as expected. It was during this migration that I got deep into the modelling of derivatives — swaps, swaptions, bond futures and the like — and that's really how I built up my hands-on asset and instrument knowledge. That project pulled me onto the analytics delivery team — originally a separate risk team and asset team that were combined — which owns asset valuation and the risk/ALM analytics. They approached me to join, and it's my current team. I also earned my CFA during this period.

Day to day now, our clients are primarily US and UK pension funds. We take their data from the custodian and model it in the platform — assets, liabilities, and member data all come together in the PFaroe DB module, which generates the risk and ALM analytics for the balance sheet. I design and run the stress and custom-scenario analysis — building bespoke scenarios to a client's specific concerns — and I advise them on what's driving the changes in their risk composition quarter over quarter.

I'm also part of our model-governance function, operating within a formal validation framework — independently reviewing and challenging models, validating their economic defensibility, benchmarking and assessing model performance, and maintaining documentation to validation standards like SR 11-7 and OSFI E-23. I hold delegated sign-off authority on valuation, sensitivity and risk outputs for multi-asset institutional portfolios ranging $5–25bn per engagement — cumulatively covering ~$50bn of client assets — reviewing curve construction, spread calibration and cross-asset interactions before release, and escalating anything that isn't economically defensible.

**Moody's — agentic-AI / tech shift (past year)**

Over the past year I've been heavily involved in agentic-AI solutions at Moody's, so my work has shifted somewhat toward the tech side. Through that I've gained real depth in the typical DevOps workflow, app development, and project and product management. I've built two internal tools in Python: an internal platform that automates our valuation cycle — cutting it by about 30% — and a cash-flow model for liquidity-analysis purposes. Right now I'm collaborating with our structured-finance team on an agentic solution for servicing clients — which is quite different from traditional software with a UI: instead, AI agents handle the client requests automatically, rather than a person doing it manually.

**Why HOOPP**

What draws me to HOOPP is that it brings all of these threads together — instrument-level risk modelling across derivatives and the whole book, inside the risk system, working right alongside the trading and portfolio teams, at one of Canada's strongest pension plans. And candidly, HOOPP isn't an unknown to me — back in my Ortec consulting days I had the privilege of working with the fund directly, so I already have a feel for how you approach risk and the balance sheet. I've spent years serving asset owners like HOOPP from the outside; the step I want to make now is to own the models inside one — and HOOPP is at the top of that list.
